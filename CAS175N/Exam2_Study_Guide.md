# Part 1 — 概念术语（中英文对照精炼版）

> 说明：带有引用标记的定义，直接出自你上传的课程章节 PDF（或与其内容一致的教材表述）；未加引用的为课堂常见术语的标准定义与用法。

**Propaganda（宣传/传播操控）**

* **EN:** Systematic attempts to shape perceptions, manipulate cognitions, and direct behavior to achieve a response that furthers the propagandist’s intent.（课堂教材强调宣传并非天生“好/坏”，关键在目的、手段与效果。）
* **中:** 通过系统化讯息与技术，塑造受众看法、影响认知并引导行为，以达成传播者的目的；本身不必然善恶，取决于目的与方法及其影响。

**Propaganda & Persuasion（宣传与劝服的关系）**

* **EN:** Both aim to influence; persuasion often allows free choice, while propaganda typically embeds strategic control (e.g., one-to-many messaging, agenda setting).
* **中:** 二者都影响受众；劝服相对强调自愿选择，宣传更常配合议程设置等策略性控制（尤其是一对多的大众传播）。

**Public opinion（公众舆论）**

* **EN:** When individual opinions on a topic are held by a significant portion of **a** public; it is the “measuring stick of power.” If opinion is for you, you have power; if against, power fades. 
* **中:** 当某议题上的个人意见被某个「公众」中相当比例的人所共有，即形成公众舆论；它是权力的“量尺”——顺之则强，逆之则弱。

**Edward Bernays’s theory of propaganda（伯内斯的宣传理论）**

* **EN:** Modern PR uses psychological insights (Freud) to manage consent by engineering needs/desires via media, symbols, and opinion leaders.
* **中:** 以心理学（弗洛伊德）为底层逻辑，经由媒体、符号与意见领袖“制造同意”，把人们的欲望与需求导向指定目标。

**Facts / Opinions（事实 / 意见）**

* **EN:** Facts can be proven true/false; opinions are evaluations/feelings about facts; boundaries blur in real life. 
* **中:** 事实可被验证真假；意见是对事实的感受与评价；现实中边界常被情绪与语境模糊。

**Doxa（常识/习见）**

* **EN:** Socially constructed “what goes without saying” common sense that carries strong rhetorical power. 
* **中:** 由社会建构的“不言自明”的常识体系，具有强大的修辞力量。

**Truth / lie / bullshit（真/假/胡诌）**

* **EN:** Truth aligns with facts; lies contradict facts; “bullshit” is indifferent to truth—aimed at impression/impact rather than accuracy.
* **中:** 真相符合事实；谎言与事实相反；“胡诌”并不在乎真假，核心在于制造印象与效果。

**Identification（认同）**

* **EN:** Audience aligns with a communicator or narrative character, making them more receptive to ideas/actions.（叙事中常与“移情/代入”并用。） 
* **中:** 受众与传播者/角色建立“自我—他者”的相似与连结，从而更易接受其理念或行为倡议。

**Stasis（停滞点/争点分析）** & **Four stasis questions（四个争点）**

* **EN:** Classical issue analysis—Fact/Conjecture? Definition? Quality (seriousness/costs)? Policy (what should/shouldn’t be done)? 
* **中:** 古典论证定位：事实/臆测？定义为何？性质与严重性？应采取何种政策/行动？

**Selective exposure（选择性接触）**

* **EN:** Preference for attitude-consistent information; a driver of polarization and “echo chambers,” esp. online. 
* **中:** 倾向接触与自己立场一致的信息；在社交媒体中易造成“信息茧房/回音室”。

**Agenda setting（议程设置）**

* **EN:** Media attention to an issue begets heightened public concern; a zero-sum allocation of public attention. 
* **中:** 媒体对议题的聚焦提升公众对其重要性的认知；社会关注度呈“此消彼长”。

**Framing & techniques（框架与技巧）**

* **EN:** How an issue is presented (the “window” that limits/angles what’s seen) alters interpretation; framing = selecting, emphasizing, and linking elements. 
* **中:** 通过“取景框”式呈现（取舍、凸显与关联信息）改变意义建构与判断。

**Priming（启动/预设评判标准）**

* **EN:** By making some considerations salient (via agenda + frames), media change the standards people use to evaluate leaders/policies. 
* **中:** 媒体把某些要点凸显，从而“预设/激活”公众评估政治人物与政策的尺⼨。

**Confirmation bias（确认性偏误）**

* **EN:** Tendency to seek/weight congruent evidence and counterargue dissonant information.（与选择性接触、偏向性处理、否认证据相关。） 
* **中:** 倾向搜寻与偏好支持既有立场的信息，同时对不利信息挑刺、反驳。

**Anger Activism Model（愤怒—行动模型）**

* **EN:** Anger increases deeper processing and action **when** efficacy is high—people must believe change is possible and they’re able to act. 
* **中:** 当个体相信“有效且可为”时，愤怒会提升精细加工并促进行动。

**Characteristics of a narrative（叙事的特征）**

* **EN:** Events, characters, and settings in meaningful, often causal sequences; Ryan’s “toolkit” (entities, change, non-habitual events, purposeful action, causal links, interpretability). 
* **中:** 叙事包含事件—角色—情境及它们的因果/时序联系；可被受众理解并产生意义。

**Psychological transportation（心理迁入/沉浸）**

* **EN:** Attentional and emotional absorption into a story world that lowers counter-arguing and enhances persuasion. 
* **中:** 沉浸到叙事世界，注意力被占用、降低反驳，从而更易受影响。

**Fallacy（谬误）**

* **EN/中:** 推理或论证中的系统性错误（如稻草人、虚假两难、因果倒置等），会在形式或内容上误导判断。

**Celebrity endorsements（名人背书）**

* **EN:** Leverages parasocial liking/trust; reduces reactance, shifts perceived norms.（与“拟社会关系/模仿”相关。） 
* **中:** 借名人好感与信任降低心理抗拒，改变“大家都这么做/想”的社会规范感。

**Glittering generalities（华丽空话）**

* **EN/中:** 用“美好但空泛”的高频关键词（如自由、繁荣、梦想）唤起正向情绪，却不提供可检验的具体含义与证据。

**Bandwagon appeal（从众/搭顺风车）**

* **EN/中:** 借“多数人都在做/想”施压，诱发“错失/被排斥”的社群焦虑以推动跟随。

**Name calling（标签抹黑）**

* **EN/中:** 用贬损性词语把对手与负面特质捆绑，转移注意力，弱化对方论点。

**Loaded words（情绪化措辞）**

* **EN/中:** 带强烈情绪色彩的词语（如“毒害”、“背叛”）用来触发情绪而非探讨事实。

**Card stacking（摘樱桃/片面陈述）**

* **EN/中:** 只呈现有利证据、遮蔽不利信息以制造“证据优势”的错觉。

**Misleading numbers（误导性数字）**

* **EN/中:** 通过错误分母、选择性区间、图形坐标截断等方式，用“看似客观”的数据误导结论。

**Stereotyping（刻板印象）**

* **EN/中:** 用过度简化的群体特征描述来推断个体或政策，常导致偏见与错误归因。

**Enemyship（敌人化/敌对想象）**

* **EN/中:** 把对象建构为“威胁性的他者”，以激发恐惧、愤怒和群体团结。

**Plain folks appeals（普通人诉求）**

* **EN/中:** 把传播者描绘为“和你一样的普通人”，以获得亲近感与信任。

**Fear appeals（恐惧诉求）**

* **EN:** Threat → fear arousal → action **if** efficacy (response + self-) is high; too much fear without efficacy backfires. 
* **中:** 威胁信息唤起恐惧，若“办法可行、我能做到”，才会促进行动；恐惧过强且缺乏效能会适得其反。

**Collective guilt / pride（集体愧疚 / 集体自豪）**

* **EN:** Group-based emotions influence out-group help or in-group bias; collective guilt可提升对受害群体的补偿支持，collective pride也可能提高同情，但也可能伴随排他性民族主义。 
* **中:** 以群体身份体验的情绪会影响跨群体态度与行为；集体愧疚常促使补偿性支持，集体自豪既能增强同理，也可能滋生排他。

# Part 2 — 简答题（中英对照，考场可直接写）

## Q1. How does propaganda influence public opinion, and what are the consequences for a democracy like ours?
## 宣传如何影响公众舆论？这对我们这样的民主国家有何影响？

**EN (≈1–2 paragraphs):**
Propaganda shapes public opinion by **setting the agenda** (what to think about), **framing** issues (how to think about them), and **priming** the standards people later use to judge leaders and policies. When media and elites concentrate attention on select problems, public concern rises at the expense of others (agenda-setting). The same facts then get **framed** to emphasize particular meanings, and repeated exposure **primes** evaluative criteria (e.g., “lives saved vs. lives lost”), which changes how citizens rate governments or candidates. 

In a democracy, these processes can be used for legitimate persuasion or for manipulative propaganda. They can **fragment publics** into echo chambers via selective exposure, distort priorities, and let elites **manufacture consent**, shifting power toward those who control attention and frames. Because public opinion is the “measuring stick of power,” sustained propaganda can tilt the policy agenda and electoral outcomes, often without citizens noticing how their evaluative yardsticks were pre-set.  

**中（≈1–2段）：**
宣传通过三步影响舆论：**议程设置**决定“公众要想什么”、**框架**决定“公众如何想”、**启动**决定“公众用什么尺⼨去评判人和政策”。当媒体/精英把注意力集中在少数议题上，公众对其重视度随之上升（议程设置）；相同事实在不同框架下产生不同意义；反复暴露又会**预设**评判标准，从而改变公众对政府或候选人的打分。 

在民主政体中，这既可能是正当劝服，也可能沦为操控：它会**分裂信息环境**、扭曲问题优先级，并让掌握注意力与框架者“制造同意”。由于公众舆论是“权力的量尺”，持续的宣传可在不显眼的前提下偏转政策议程与选举结果。

## Q2. What were Sigmund Freud’s claims/findings regarding the human mind, and how were his ideas used in propaganda/marketing in the U.S.?
## 西格蒙德·弗洛伊德关于人类思维的主张/发现是什么？他的思想是如何在美国的宣传/营销中运用的？

**EN:**
Freud argued that human behavior is strongly driven by **unconscious motives** (desires, fears, identity-related defenses). Modern PR—especially **Edward Bernays**—translated this into practice: instead of only giving facts, campaigns **engineered desire** (symbols, lifestyle associations, opinion leaders) so that people would feel an urge to buy or support without experiencing it as coercion. In U.S. marketing and political communication, this meant appealing to deep-seated needs (status, belonging, security) and normalizing them through mass media rituals and celebrity cues—an early recipe for “managing consent.”
**中：**
弗洛伊德认为人的行为深受**无意识动机**（欲望、恐惧与自我防御）的驱使。其外甥伯内斯把这一思想转化为现代公关操作：不只讲事实，而是**“制造欲望”**——借助符号、生活方式联想与意见领袖，使大众在“不觉得被强迫”的情况下自发购买或支持。在美国的营销与政治传播中，这转化为对地位、归属、安全等深层需求的持续召唤，并通过大众媒体与名人背书将其常态化，以“管理同意”。

## Q3. According to the textbook, propaganda is neither inherently good nor bad. What makes propaganda “good,” and how would you describe it?
## 根据教科书，宣传本身无好坏之分。是什么让宣传变得“好”？你会如何描述它？

**EN:**
“Good” propaganda is **effective** (achieves clear, verifiable goals with appropriate audience targeting and message-strategy fit) **and** **ethically defensible** (truthful enough for informed judgment, respects audience autonomy/efficacy, minimizes unjust harm). Technically, it integrates agenda-setting, framing, and credible messengers to mobilize action while providing workable efficacy (“what to do” and “I can do it”), avoiding deception that would corrode public trust.
**中：**
“好的”宣传一方面要**有效**（目标清晰可检验、受众—讯息—媒介匹配），另一方面应**合乎伦理**（信息足够真实以供判断、尊重受众自主与效能、避免不公正伤害）。在技术上，它善用议程设置、框架与可信代言来动员行动，并提供切实的**效能感**（做什么/我能做），同时避免损害公信力的误导与欺骗。
