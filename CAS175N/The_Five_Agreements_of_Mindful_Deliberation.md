The Five Agreements of Mindful Deliberation  
正念审议的五项共识

(from Jeremy David Engels, On Mindful Democracy: A Declaration of Interdependence to Mend a Fractured World (Parallax Press, 2026, forthcoming)  
（引自Jeremy David Engels，《正念民主：修复分裂世界的相互依存宣言》，Parallax出版社，2026年即将出版）

“We are all in this together.”  
“我们同在一条船上。”

This is a deliberation, not a debate, and the outcome affects us all. We win, or lose, together.  
这是一场审议，不是辩论，其结果影响到我们每一个人。我们共同赢，也共同输。

It’s vital to identify all the stakeholders (including non-humans) who will be affected by our decision and to determine what the impact will be, for them as well as for those of us in the room.  
必须识别所有受决策影响的利益相关者（包括非人类），并评估决策对他们以及在场每个人的影响。

“Speak up! And listen up!”  
“请大胆发言，也要认真倾听！”

Everyone is invited to talk—we need your perspective, and your wisdom—but no one should dominate the conversation.  
每个人都被邀请发言——我们需要你的观点和智慧——但不应有人主导对话。

Mindful listening is as important as talking. Everyone is equal in the moment of address. Everyone participating is of equal worth.  
用心倾听与发言同等重要。每个人在发言时都是平等的，所有参与者都同样有价值。

Make space for those who seem hesitant to speak up and for those who have been excluded in the past.  
要为那些犹豫发言的人和曾被排除在外的人留出空间。

“Stay on Topic.”  
“紧扣主题。”

Deliberation does not work if some people talk about one thing while others talk about something else.  
如果有人谈论这个，有人谈论那个，审议就无法进行。

A deliberating community must constantly remember to bring its collective attention back to the topic under consideration, and to the present moment.  
审议群体必须时刻提醒自己，将集体注意力拉回到当前议题和当下时刻。

In deliberation, we identify the challenge or issue that brings us together today as clearly as possible and stick to it, letting old ghosts, resentments, tangents, and red herrings lie.  
在审议中，我们要尽可能清晰地界定让我们聚在一起的挑战或问题，并始终聚焦于此，放下旧怨、偏题和无关话题。

“Practice the five questions.”  
“践行五问法。”

Deliberation is not an academic exercise. It is a practice with real-world consequences.  
审议不是学术练习，而是有现实后果的实践。

When you speak up, you must give reasons for your beliefs, support those reasons with the best, most credible evidence you can muster, and make arguments based on logic and evidence.  
发言时，你必须为自己的观点提供理由，并用最有力、最可信的证据支持这些理由，基于逻辑和证据进行论证。

To say, “This is my truth,” without any other support, or to say, “I feel this deeply in my heart, so it must be true,” is not good enough in a deliberation—the well-being of the community is at stake.  
仅仅说“这就是我的真理”或“我内心深信，所以一定正确”是不够的——因为这关系到整个群体的福祉。

That’s why we practice the five questions: they transform opinions into arguments, and arguments into wisdom:  
这就是我们践行五问法的原因：它能将观点转化为论证，将论证转化为智慧：

    • What are the pros of this position?  
      这个立场的优点是什么？
    • What are the cons?  
      缺点是什么？
    • What are the unknowns and the questions we are not equipped to answer at this point?  
      目前有哪些未知和我们无法回答的问题？
    • What quality evidence do we have to support or oppose this position/policy (remembering a personal opinion or a gut feeling by itself is not quality evidence)?  
      我们有哪些高质量证据支持或反对这一立场/政策？（请记住，个人观点或直觉本身不是高质量证据）
    • And, finally, what will we do if we are wrong?  
      最后，如果我们错了，我们该怎么办？

“It’s Okay to Change Your Mind.”  
“改变想法是可以的。”

As you listen and learn, it’s okay to change your mind. This is not “flip-flopping.” It’s education, and education is always good.  
在倾听和学习的过程中，改变想法是可以的。这不是“反复无常”，而是教育，而教育总是好的。

It is a stepping stone to wisdom, connection, and transformation.  
这是通向智慧、联系与转变的垫脚石。
